Most parents who bring a child to us for adenoids describe the same picture. The child snores. They sleep with their mouth open. They wake up tired, struggle to concentrate at school, and seem to catch every cold going around. Somewhere along the way, a scan put a number on it — 80%, 90%, sometimes more — and surgery was suggested.

## What adenoids actually are

Adenoids are a patch of lymphatic tissue sitting where the back of the nose meets the throat. They are part of a child's immune defence, catching what gets breathed in. They naturally grow through early childhood and usually shrink on their own by the teenage years.

The problem is not that they exist. It is when repeated infection or persistent allergy keeps them swollen, and swollen tissue in that particular spot blocks the airway behind the nose.

## The signs worth noticing

- Snoring most nights, not only during a cold
- Sleeping with the mouth open, and waking with a dry mouth
- Restless sleep, or brief pauses in breathing
- A nasal quality to the voice
- Daytime tiredness, irritability or trouble concentrating
- Frequent ear infections or dulled hearing

The sleep disturbance is the part that tends to get overlooked. A child who breathes through their mouth all night is not sleeping deeply, and the effects show up as behaviour and attention problems long before anyone connects them to the nose.

## When to have it looked at

Get it checked if snoring is persistent rather than occasional, if you notice pauses in breathing, if there is recurring ear trouble, or if a teacher raises concentration concerns alongside any of the above.

Bring the scan or scope report with you if one has already been done. It tells us how much of the airway is actually obstructed, which matters more than the child's symptoms alone.

## Where homoeopathy fits

Homoeopathic treatment approaches enlarged adenoids by treating the child's overall tendency — the pattern of recurring infection and allergic reaction that keeps the tissue inflamed — rather than the swelling in isolation. Treatment is reviewed at every follow-up and adjusted as things change.

Improvement, where it happens, is usually gradual: the snoring eases, sleep settles, colds become less frequent. Many parents tell us the change in sleep is what they notice first.
