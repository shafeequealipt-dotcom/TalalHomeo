Every year around the same time, our consulting room fills with the same complaint: the sneezing starts at dawn, ten or fifteen in a row, then a blocked nose that stays heavy all day. It eases in the dry months and returns with the rains.

## Why the coast makes it worse

Kochi's humidity rarely drops. Damp indoor air is ideal for two things that drive allergic rhinitis: house dust mites, which thrive above roughly 60% relative humidity, and mould spores, which bloom on walls, in bathrooms and behind furniture through the monsoon.

Add the sea air, the dust stirred up between showers, and homes shut tight against the rain, and the exposure runs continuously for weeks.

## What separates it from a cold

A cold builds over a day or two, often with a sore throat or mild fever, and clears within a week or so. Allergic rhinitis switches on abruptly — usually on waking or on entering a particular room — runs clear rather than thick, and comes with itching in the nose, eyes or palate. No fever. And it recurs on the same schedule every year.

## Practical things that help

- Wash bedding weekly in hot water; dust mites live in mattresses and pillows, not in the air
- Air rooms out on dry mornings, even briefly
- Deal with damp patches and wall mould early — it is a source, not just a stain
- Dry clothes outside where possible; indoor drying raises humidity sharply
- Wipe surfaces with a damp cloth rather than dusting them into the air

None of this is a cure. It reduces the load, which is worth doing regardless of what treatment you are on.

## The homoeopathic view

Rather than suppressing each flare as it arrives, treatment looks at why the reaction is set so sensitively in the first place — and at the pattern of your particular version of it. Two patients with identical diagnoses often need entirely different remedies, which is why the consultation asks about far more than the nose.

If you have had allergy testing, bring the report. Bring your current antihistamines or nasal sprays too — treatment is planned alongside them, and nothing should be stopped abruptly on your own.

If breathing becomes difficult, or if wheezing develops, that needs to be seen promptly rather than managed at home.
